CC4. Replication. Recreate, then improve, someone else’s chart

I replicated a Guardian chart comparing poverty in the US and China. The original used separate scales to suggest that poverty reduction in the US failed compared to China, due to policy choices.

The original comparison was misleading because it ignored the starting baselines. By merging the data onto a single shared axis, I revealed the true context: US poverty has remained historically low, whereas China’s "success" was a reduction from extreme poverty levels. This correction challenges the article's narrative by demonstrating that the two nations operate on vastly different economic scales and political regimes, making a direct "policy choice" comparison statistically flawed.

CC5. API and Scraper

I used the World Bank API to track remittance inflows to Mexico, India, and the Philippines (1974-2024). This chart highlights the massive growth in money flowing to the Global South over the last 50 years.

API Description

Base URL: https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/

Parameters: To call this data, I appended the country codes (MX;IN;PH), the specific indicator ID for personal remittances, and the format parameter.

URL:
https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/MX;IN;PH/indicator/BX.TRF.PWKR.CD.DT?format=json&per_page=500
