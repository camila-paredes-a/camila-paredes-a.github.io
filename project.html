Understanding the Impact of Income and Benefits on Family Wellbeing in the UK

This project explores how household income, housing costs, and social support programs affect disposable income for families with children. It focuses on lower-middle income families and evaluates how parenthood shapes household budgets across income groups and regions from 2003 to 2024. The analysis aims to identify patterns, highlight policy gaps, and provide insights for policymakers.

CC1. Housing Costs

We examine additional weekly housing costs incurred by households after having children. Chart 1 shows the difference between households with and without children, highlighting the financial impact of parenthood.

CC2. Housing Affordability

This chart explores housing as a share of total household spending before and after parenthood. The data reveals how housing pressures disproportionately affect low-income households.

CC3. Residual Income by Region

After accounting for housing costs, residual income varies across regions and income quintiles. Chart 3 identifies regions where families are most financially stretched.

CC4. Residual Income After Housing

This chart illustrates that higher gross income does not always translate into higher disposable income for lower-middle income families (£300–£400/week). Housing costs and tapered benefits flatten net income growth. The red band highlights this income range, showing families with minimal net gain despite higher earnings.

CC5. Policy Gaps & Non-Claimants

Chart 4.1 focuses on families eligible but not claiming benefits, providing insights into gaps in policy coverage and awareness.

CC6. Analytical Patterns

Chart 5 visualises patterns across regions, income levels, housing costs, and Child Benefit uptake. This combined analysis helps identify correlations and policy-relevant trends.

CC7. Family Size Distribution

Chart 6 compares family size distribution for families claiming and not claiming Child Benefit. This analysis highlights whether larger families are more likely to access social support.

CC8. Big Data Analysis

Future coding challenges will explore housing stress over time using larger datasets and additional income and benefits data.

CC9. Interactive Visualisations

Interactive elements allow users to explore regional disparities and policy impacts in depth.

CC10. Advanced Analysis & ML

Advanced visual analyses and machine learning techniques extend the project to predictive insights, exploring income, benefits, and residual income dynamics.

Conclusion

The analysis shows that for lower-middle income families, higher gross income does not always lead to higher disposable income due to housing costs and benefit tapering. Policy levers strongly shape household wellbeing, highlighting the need for targeted interventions to support financially stretched families.
