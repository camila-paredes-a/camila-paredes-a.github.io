PP434: New Breadline Project

Project Overview

This project investigates the financial pressures faced by households with children in the UK. We focus on housing costs, residual income, and social support programs like Child Benefit. The goal is to understand how parenthood affects household budgets across income groups and regions from 2003 to 2024.

The project uses weekly coding challenges (CC1–CC10) to build visualisations that reveal patterns, inequalities, and policy-relevant insights. Each week focuses on a different aspect, from housing affordability to family size distribution and benefit uptake.

CC1 & CC2: Housing Costs

We begin by examining additional weekly housing costs incurred by households after having children. Chart 1 shows the difference between households with and without children, highlighting the financial impact of parenthood.

Chart 2 explores housing affordability, showing housing as a share of total household spending before and after parenthood. The data reveals how housing pressures can disproportionately affect low-income households.

CC3: Residual Income After Housing

After accounting for housing costs, we assess residual income by region. Chart 3 shows how disposable income varies across different parts of the UK and income quintiles, helping identify regions most financially stretched.

CC4 & CC5: Child Benefit Uptake

Child Benefit is a key support for families. Chart 4 illustrates the percentage of families receiving the benefit across regions. Chart 4.1 focuses on families eligible but not claiming, providing insights into gaps in policy coverage and awareness.

CC6: Analytical Patterns Across Regions and Income

Chart 5 visualises patterns across regions, income levels, housing costs, and Child Benefit uptake. This combined analysis helps identify correlations and policy-relevant trends.

CC7: Family Size Distribution

Chart 6 compares family size distribution for families claiming and not claiming Child Benefit. This helps us understand whether larger families are more likely to access social support.

CC8–CC10: Additional Analyses

Future coding challenges explore complementary angles such as housing stress over time, regional disparities, and advanced visual analyses. Charts 7–10 will extend the project to include these insights.
